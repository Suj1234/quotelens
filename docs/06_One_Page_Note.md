**The thesis.** The comparison table is not the hard problem. Several products already pull prices out of PDFs and spreadsheets. The hard problem is that two buyers given the same five responses produce two different spreadsheets, because each silently decides what "per box" means, which FX rate to use, whether a footnote discount applies, and whether "same as last year" is a number. The product's job is to make those decisions explicit, reviewable and auditable — to show the buyer what it is *not* sure about rather than hide it behind an accuracy claim. Everything below follows from that.

**What I built.** One flow: co-pilot drafts the RFx → dispatch by real email (Gmail SMTP/IMAP; a mock mode exists) → responses in any format → a six-stage pipeline (classify, extract, map, normalise, questionnaire, flags) → a grid where every cell has one of eight states and a provenance chain back to the cell, page region, sentence or photo crop it came from → a review queue for everything below threshold → an assumptions ledger → a question panel that answers by writing and running SQL over the normalised data and shows the query → saved scenarios → an award memo PDF that carries the ledger and the open items. An eval page scores the app against a 150-cell answer key: **[N]/150** correct or honestly flagged on the seed set at submission; the misses are listed on the page, not hidden.

**What the ugly edges do.** Angled photo: per-cell confidence; the obscured price is queued with the crop beside it, excluded from totals until confirmed. 27 of 30 lines: "not quoted" is a state, the vendor drops out of those lines in per-line awards, and the question layer says so. USD: converted at a dated rate that lives in the ledger and can be changed; a sensitivity question reruns the query at ±3%. "Per bundle" with no bundle size for four items: amber, best guess shown, one-click "Ask vendor"; the vendor's reply revealed two of the four guesses would have been 20–25% wrong. Footnote "rates net of 2.5% if paid in 10 days": since we pay at 45 days the payable price is printed ÷ 0.975 — roughly ₹11 lakh a year that a copy-paste would miss. "Rest same as last year": flagged, never invented.

**Decisions and why.** *Grid first, chat beside it* — buyers sign off on grids; chat is the question layer, not the surface. *Answers are computed, not narrated* — the brief forbids faked reasoning, and a query is checkable. *Reading and deciding are separate layers* — Gemini reads documents and writes SQL; a decision layer built to TypeSafe Jev's typed-question interface (choice / score / yes-no with probabilities) does classification, line mapping and questionnaire judgments. It runs on Gemini in this build and switches to Jev with one key; every decision is labelled with which engine made it. *No agent framework* — a sequence of typed calls is easier to debug and to explain than a runtime. *Real email at zero cost* — Gmail, with the webhook path stubbed. *Two seeded users, no auth* — the hours went to extraction.

**What I left out, deliberately.** Supplier discovery, negotiation, PO creation and ERP integration (other jobs). A historical-pricing store — "same as last year" is handled by asking, not remembering; the next version should remember. Prompt-optimisation tooling (DSPy) — I built an eval instead, which is the half of that loop worth having in 48 hours. Voice input. Mobile. A vendor-side portal where suppliers see what we extracted and correct it — this is the feature I'd build next, because it moves the review work to the party that knows the answer.

**What broke, honestly.** [Fill after build from PROGRESS.md — e.g. the prose-format Word letter merged items until the prompt said "one item per price"; the rotated phone photo needed orientation detection before extraction; line mapping on the vendor who used generic descriptions needed size and ply to outweigh text.] Each of these is visible in the repo's DECISIONS.md.

**Competitive context.** Fairmarkit, Quotable and Pipefy advertise extraction and award recommendations; Keelvar has scenario analysis; the suites (Coupa, Ariba, Jaggaer) solve it by forcing vendors into a portal template. None of the public material shows the buyer per-cell uncertainty or a chain from a number to its source. Aerchain's own sourcing agent accepts bids in any format — so the differentiation I'd argue for is not "we parse", it's "we show the buyer where parsing is a judgment call, and we make the judgment reversible".

**Where the interesting problem was.** Normalisation, not extraction. The ledger is the product.
